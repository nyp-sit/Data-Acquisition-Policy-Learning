1Overview

TD-MPC learns a model of the world and the reward, then plans actions with it. lerobot's TD-MPC has three requirements that a normal Lab recording does not meet:

TD-MPC needsA normal Lab recording hasHow this lab solves it
One cameraUsually two (front + wrist)The conversion script keeps only front
Square images (84 × 84 is the design size)640 × 480The script center-crops to 480 × 480 and resizes to 84 × 84
A reward on every frame (next.reward)No rewardYou end every successful episode in a held success pose; the script gives those last frames reward 1.0

The whole lab follows this pipeline. The recording itself is a normal Lab recording; only step 6 is TD-MPC-specific.

2Prepare the computer

Do these once. Each one fixes a failure you would otherwise hit partway through the lab.

2.1 Apply the Windows training update

Without it, a local training run fails on Windows. Follow updatewin\README.md, or from the repository root:

cd C:\makermodslab
git apply updatewin\windows-training-fix.patch

2.2 Install the GPU build of PyTorch

Close MakerMods Lab first, then:

uv pip install --python .venv\Scripts\python.exe torch==2.11.0 torchvision==0.26.0 `
  --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu126 `
  --reinstall-package torch --reinstall-package torchvision

.venv\Scripts\python.exe -c "import torch; print(torch.cuda.is_available(), torch.cuda.get_device_name(0))"

The check must print True and your GPU's name.

2.3 Allow checkpoint links: run the Lab as Administrator

When lerobot saves a checkpoint, it creates a checkpoints\last symbolic link. Windows only allows that for an Administrator, or with Developer Mode turned on. Without one of them, training crashes at the first checkpoint with OSError: [WinError 1314] A required privilege is not held by the client.

Do one of these:

  • Option A (recommended): Settings → System → For developers → turn on Developer Mode. This works for every terminal.
  • Option B: Start the Lab from a terminal opened with Run as administrator.

2.4 Start MakerMods Lab

cd C:\makermodslab
.venv\Scripts\activate
makermodslab

Open http://localhost:8000. Log in to Hugging Face if you want datasets named after your account; it is optional for this lab.

3Set up the scene and camera

3.1 Choose a simple task

TD-MPC learns best from a short, repeatable task with a clear finished state. This lab uses:

Task“Pick up the cube and place it in the target square.” Done means: the cube is inside the square, and the gripper is open and raised above it.

Mark the target square with tape. Use the same cube, table surface and lighting for every episode.

3.2 Place the front camera

The conversion keeps only the center square of the camera image and throws away 80 pixels on the left and right. Anything outside that square is invisible to TD-MPC.

  • Fix the camera to the table or a stand; it must not move between episodes.
  • Center the arm and the task area horizontally in the camera image.
  • Keep the operator, the leader arm and your hands outside the center square.
  • Use a plain background, steady light and no window glare.
  • Leave the camera at its native 640 × 480. Most USB cameras refuse a square resolution, so the script crops instead.

4Set up the robot in the Lab

  1. Use the robot menu in the top-right corner → Robot settings → create a robot (SO-101, single arm).
  2. Detect the leader and follower ports and calibrate both arms, as the Lab guides you.
  3. In the robot's camera section, add one camera:
    FieldValueWhy
    NamefrontThe script looks for observation.images.front
    Resolution640 × 480Supported by almost every USB camera
    FPS30The reward window below assumes 30 fps
    A second camera (for example wrist) is allowed. The script ignores it, and it keeps the same recording usable for ACT or SmolVLA later.
  4. Run a short teleoperation to check that the follower copies the leader smoothly and the camera preview shows the scene from Figure 2.
CheckpointThe robot shows as ready, teleoperation works, and the camera preview has the task centered.

5Record the dataset

This is the most important part of the lab. TD-MPC learns its reward from how each episode ends, so you will record two kinds of episode on purpose, and end both of them the same way:

  • Success episodes (about 50): the cube ends inside the square, and you hold still for 2 seconds at the end.
  • Failure episodes (10, planned in advance): the cube ends outside the square, and you hold still for 2 seconds at the end too.

Practise the success motion a few times in teleoperation (step 4) until you can do it smoothly in about 12 seconds.

5.2 The episode cycle and its controls

After you press start, the session repeats the same two phases for every episode: an orange Reset phase (nothing is recorded) and a Recording phase. The follower copies the leader arm in both phases.

ButtonKeyWhenWhat it does
DoneSpace or →RecordingEnds and saves the episode. Press it only after the 2-second hold.
Re-recordDelRecording onlyThrows the current take away. After a reset, the same episode number is recorded again. It does not work after you have pressed Done.
Start Next EpisodeSpaceResetSkips the rest of the reset countdown once the cube and the arm are back in place.
Pause / ResumeEnterReset (or armed during Recording)Freezes the reset countdown, for example when the cube has rolled away. Pressed during Recording, it takes effect at the next reset.
Finish sessionEscAny timeEnds the session and keeps every saved episode (see 5.7).

5.3 A success episode, step by step

Do every success episode the same way, in one smooth attempt, with these six steps:

StepWhat you do with the leader armCheck
1 HomeThe episode starts with the arm in your home pose (the same pose every time, set during Reset) and the cube on its start mark. Stay still for about 1 second.Cube on its mark; hands out of the camera's center square
2 ReachOpen the gripper and move it straight to about 5 cm above the cube, in one movement. Don't search back and forth.Gripper centered over the cube
3 GraspLower until the jaws are on both sides of the cube, then close the gripper firmly. Pause for half a second.Cube held, not pushed sideways
4 Lift & carryLift about 5 cm and move straight to above the center of the square. Don't drag the cube along the table.Cube still in the gripper
5 Place & openLower until the cube touches the table inside the square, then open the gripper fully.Cube entirely inside the tape
6 Raise & holdRaise the open gripper about 5 cm straight up, then freeze. Count “one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two”, then press Done (Space).Nothing moves for 2 seconds
It counts as a success only if all of these are true when you press Done The cube is entirely inside the tape · the gripper is open and not touching the cube · the arm is raised · nothing has moved for 2 seconds · no hand is in the camera's center square.
Press Done without moving the armKeep holding the leader arm with your working hand and press Space with the other hand, or have a partner press it. If you let go of the leader arm it can sag, and the follower would copy that movement into the frames that get the reward.
Watch the timerAim to be holding the finish by about 15 seconds. If the timer passes about 17 seconds and you are not holding the finish yet, press Re-record (Del). At 20 seconds the episode is saved automatically, and a success episode without a held finish teaches TD-MPC the wrong thing.

Why the hold matters: the conversion script (step 6) gives reward 1.0 to the last 30 frames (1 second) of every success episode and 0.0 to every other frame. Holding for 2 seconds makes sure that last second shows only the finished, still scene, even if you are a little slow to press Done.

5.4 Failure episodes

Failure episodes show TD-MPC what “not done” looks like. Without them, every episode in the dataset ends with reward 1.0, and the model learns that the reward comes at the end of the episode, not from where the cube is.

Perform a failure exactly like a success, with the same smooth motion, the same raise and the same 2-second hold, except for one deliberate mistake. The cube's position at the end should be the only difference between a success and a failure:

TypeHow to perform itHow it ends
A · ShortDo steps 1–4 normally, but set the cube down 3–5 cm before the square.Steps 5–6 as usual: open the gripper, raise it, hold for 2 s, press Done. Then write the episode on the log sheet.
B · OvershootCarry the cube past the square and set it down 3–5 cm beyond it.
C · Missed graspLower the gripper 1–2 cm to the side of the cube and close it on nothing. Leave the cube where it is and do the rest of the motion as if you were holding it: carry to the square, open, raise.
  • Make every failure obvious: the cube is completely outside the tape, never on the line.
  • Don't hesitate or jerk the arm. Move as smoothly as in a success episode.
  • Follow the plan below, so the failures are spread through the whole session.

Failure plan. Make episodes 4, 10, 16, 22, 28, 34, 40, 46, 52 and 58 (as numbered on screen) the failures, and do every other episode as a success:

6Convert the recording for TD-MPC

The script updatewin\lab_TDMPC_prepare.py reads the recording and writes a new dataset. The recording itself is never changed.

6.1 Trial run on 2 episodes

Close nothing; the Lab can stay open. In a second terminal, from the repository root, replace <your_hf_user> with the owner shown in step 5.1:

cd C:\makermodslab
.venv\Scripts\python.exe updatewin\lab_TDMPC_prepare.py `
  --src <your_hf_user>/tdmpc_pick_raw `
  --dst <your_hf_user>/tdmpc_pick_trial `
  --camera front --size 84 --success-frames 30 --max-episodes 2

Expected ending (frame counts differ):

Converting 2 episodes (1308 frames) of <your_hf_user>/tdmpc_pick_raw: camera 'front' -> 84x84, ...
  episode 0 saved
  episode 1 saved
Done: <your_hf_user>/tdmpc_pick_trial at C:\Users\<you>\.cache\huggingface\lerobot\<your_hf_user>\tdmpc_pick_trial
  episodes: 2 (0 marked failed)
  frames:   1308 (60 with reward 1.0)

A long libtorchcodec warning at the start is normal; lerobot falls back to the pyav decoder.

6.2 Full conversion

Put the “For the script” numbers of the failure rows of your log sheet (5.6) after --failed-episodes. With the plan from 5.4 and nothing removed, that is:

.venv\Scripts\python.exe updatewin\lab_TDMPC_prepare.py `
  --src <your_hf_user>/tdmpc_pick_raw `
  --dst <your_hf_user>/tdmpc_pick_84 `
  --camera front --size 84 --success-frames 30 `
  --failed-episodes 3 9 15 21 27 33 39 45 51 57
OptionMeaning
--srcThe recording from step 5
--dstName of the new TD-MPC dataset. It is saved to the lerobot cache, where the Lab lists it
--camera frontThe one camera to keep
--size 84Square image size in pixels
--success-frames 30How many final frames of a successful episode get reward 1.0 (30 = 1 s at 30 fps)
--failed-episodes …0-based numbers of the failed episodes; their reward stays 0.0
--max-episodes NConvert only the first N episodes (for trials)
--overwriteReplace an existing output dataset of the same name
--pushAlso upload the new dataset to the Hugging Face Hub

Converting about 60 episodes takes a few minutes; most of the time is spent decoding the recorded video.

CheckpointThe script prints Done, the failed count matches your log sheet, and tdmpc_pick_84 appears in My library.

8Check the result

In the run's Training logs, confirm these lines in order:

  1. 'device': 'cuda': training is on the GPU.
  2. num_learnable_params=2743279 (3M): the TD-MPC model with one 84 × 84 camera and the SO-101's 6 joints.
  3. Progress such as Training: … 2.8step/s, with the step counter moving in the Lab.
  4. Every Log frequency steps, a metrics line. The values to watch:
MetricMeaningHealthy trend
lossOverall training lossFalls fast at first, then slowly
reward_lossHow well the model predicts your rewardFalls; it stays near its start if the reward is almost all zeros (too few or too short success windows)
consistency_lossHow well the world model predicts the next stateFalls and stays low
Q, VPredicted value of statesSettle between 0 and 1 / (1 − 0.9) = 10, the discounted reward range
grdnGradient sizeComes down from its first values; sudden spikes mean unstable training

10Limitations

Deploying this policy from the Lab does not work yetThe policy expects one 84 × 84 image, but the robot's camera delivers 640 × 480. lerobot's rollout only resizes images in its HIL pipeline, and MakerMods Lab has no crop-and-resize step for a running policy. Running the policy on the arm needs a crop-and-resize step added to the rollout, which is not part of this lab.
  • Offline TD-MPC learns from what you recorded. lerobot's TD-MPC was designed for simulators that give a reward at every step and let the policy explore. With about 60 human demonstrations and an end-of-episode reward, expect a weaker policy than ACT trained on the same recording.
  • The reward is only as good as your endings. An episode that “succeeds” without holding the finished pose teaches the wrong thing.
  • 84 × 84 loses detail. Small objects and fine gripper positions may be hard to see. --size 96 or 128 are possible, but training is slower.
  • The same recording also trains ACT or SmolVLA. Train them on tdmpc_pick_raw (all cameras, no conversion) to compare, and to get a policy the Lab can deploy today.
